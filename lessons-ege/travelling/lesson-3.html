Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently performs all 4 tasks of the EGE speaking part (tasks 1–4) on the topic "travelling / holidays", reusing the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: warm-up recycling the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled (asking questions about an advert, interview) → to free monologue (comparing photos) — with growing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we practise "model → support → own performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI marking and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write out 5 phrases/idioms on the topic "travelling" from Lessons 1–2. For each, add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning.

Step 2 · Share in pairs (Pair Share, 5 min).

Partner A tells their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 clarifying question and adds a phrase the partner did not have. Then you swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • What is the best trip you have ever taken — and what made it special?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (each student writes 5 phrases ON THEIR OWN — this consolidates more strongly than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to bring the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 into active speech before the speaking tasks. Note good idioms (breathtaking view, hidden gem, step out of one's comfort zone) on the board — they will feed into tasks 3–4.
2

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it silently), then read aloud. The assessment covers phonetics: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make meaningful pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and when listing. ③ Link words (linking): an_apple, read_it. ④ Watch out for "tricky" words: comfortable, vegetable, recipe, mountain, island (silent s), determine, area, route, tourist.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common mark losses (FIPI-2024 data): a pause INSIDE a phrasal verb (looking · for) — it breaks the meaning; dropping the linking r (there‿is); stress on the prefix a- (ˈalike instead of aˈlike, aˈbove); flat intonation in a list (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); inserting/omitting articles and words not in the text.
Real FIPI text (ege_speaking.json, read, guid B6CE9DA3…). Note: the "reading aloud" text bank in the data is not tagged by topic — this is a real exam text of general content (the format of task 1 does not depend on the topic). Have them read it twice, the second time for the recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload — phonetics is marked "selectively".
3

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

Study the advert and ask 5 direct questions on the prompts (1.5 min to prepare, 20 sec per question). The assessment covers the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and how well it matches the prompt.

You are considering going on a hop-on-hop-off sightseeing tour and want to get more information. Ask about: 1) operation hours; 2) starting point; 3) tourist attractions to see; 4) price for one person; 5) group discounts.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary + subject + main verb? — What ARE the operation hours? Where DOES the tour start? CAN I get a discount? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Main task 2 mistakes (FIPI-2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a universal template (Do you have information about…?) — not counted; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixed constructions (Where is the location of…?); not asking about all 5 prompts. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EACH prompt.
Real FIPI task (ege_speaking.json, ask, guid 7BFE2AC1… — "hop-on-hop-off sightseeing tour"). Model questions (FIPI sampleQuestions): 1) What are the operation hours? 2) Where is the starting point of the tour? 3) What tourist attractions will we see? 4) How much does the tour cost for one person? 5) Do you offer group discounts? Analyse the inversion in each; have students ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question.

Task 3 · Interview (5 questions)

15 min

Answer 5 interview questions on the topic "travelling". Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer.

Questions (real FIPI topic):
  1. What is the best way of travelling around Russia?
  2. What is your most memorable trip?
  3. Why has it made such a big impression on you?
  4. What places in Russia do you think are worth visiting?
  5. What problems can people have when they travel?
Technique. No "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, mostly, because, for example and the topic idioms (breathtaking view, broaden one's horizons). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences. The verb tense in the answer = the tense of the question.
0:40
Common mark losses (FIPI-2024): a ONE-phrase answer instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer does not match the question; a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of a precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer (By train.).
Real FIPI task (ege_speaking.json, interview, guid 8F9755F3… — "travelling around Russia"). Model answer (questions 2–3, from FIPI sampleAnswers): "My most memorable trip was a journey to Lake Baikal with my family. It made such a big impression on me because the lake was breathtakingly beautiful and incredibly pure, and I had never seen nature on that scale before." Note the "answer → reason → detail" structure and the B2 vocabulary. Have them answer all 5 in 40 sec, for the recording.
5

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The most "valuable" task (max marks). Project "Summer holidays" — leave a voice message for a friend about the two photos, speak continuously for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — do ALL of them in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences between the two types of summer holiday;
  • ② 1–2 advantages of the two types of summer holidays;
  • ③ 1–2 drawbacks of the two types of summer holidays;
  • ④ your opinion: which way of spending the summer holidays you preferred as a child, and why.
Support (monologue framework):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our project on summer holidays…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that one is an active holiday and the other is a relaxing one.
• One advantage of … is that it helps you broaden your horizons / recharge your batteries…
• However, a drawback of … is that…
• As for me, as a child I preferred… because…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): K1 content — all 4 points covered; K2 organisation — introduction, logic, ending, linkers; K3 language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Main task 4 mistakes (FIPI-2024): a superficial difference (photo 1 has the sea, photo 2 has the mountains) instead of generalising about the essence of the topic (active ↔ relaxing holiday); the photos not linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a coherent monologue; switching genre to "a story"; skipping a plan point; long pauses.
Real FIPI task (ege_speaking.json, compare, guid 7BD696D0… — project "Summer holidays", two photos of a summer holiday). Teacher’s note: first build the framework together (using the support), then record the whole thing for 3 min without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak continuously. Link it to the topic: two types of summer holidays → travelling idioms from Lessons 1–2.
