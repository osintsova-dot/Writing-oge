Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently performs all 4 tasks of the EGE speaking part (tasks 1–4) on the material of the "holidays / celebrations" topic, applying the vocabulary from Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: a warm-up recirculating the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled (questions, interview) → to free monologue (comparing photos) — with increasing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we practise "model → support → own performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI marking and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write down 5 phrases on the "holidays & traditions" topic from Lessons 1–2. Add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning to each one.

Step 2 · Share in pairs (Pair Share, 5 min).

Partner A tells their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 follow-up question and adds a phrase the partner didn't have. Then swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • Which holiday do you and your friends enjoy most — and how do you celebrate it?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (everyone writes their own 5 phrases — this consolidates more strongly than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to bring the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 into active speech before the speaking tasks. Note the strong phrases on the board (festive atmosphere, gather around the table, cultural heritage) — they will feed into tasks 3–4.

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it through silently), then read aloud. The marking covers phonetics: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make meaningful pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and when listing. ③ Link words (linking): an_apple, read_it. ④ Watch out for "tricky" words: comfortable, vegetable, recipe, mountain, island (silent s), ancient, ceremony, anniversary.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it through silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common mark losses (FIPI-2024 data): a pause INSIDE a phrase (looking · for) — breaks the meaning; dropping the linking r (there‿is); wrong stress in long words (aˈnniversary, ˈceremony, comˈmemorate); flat intonation in a list (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); inserting extra articles/prepositions that aren't in the text.
Teacher’s note: let them read twice, the second time for the recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload — phonetics is corrected "spot by spot". (Real FIPI read-aloud passage; the Task 1 text is topic-neutral and marked purely on phonetics, so any genuine FIPI passage fits the lesson.)

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

You are considering celebrating your birthday in this café and want more information. Ask 5 direct questions on the points. The marking covers the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and how well it matches the point.

Birthday parties for all ages! Ask about: 1) location; 2) types of food served; 3) vegetarian dishes; 4) dancing floor; 5) group discounts.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb? — Where IS the café? What food DO you serve? IS there a dancing floor? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Main mistakes in task 2 (FIPI-2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a universal template (Do you have information about…?) — not credited; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixing constructions (Where is the location of…?); using which instead of what when there is no given set; a point not asked about. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EACH of the 5 points.
Model questions (FIPI): 1) Where is the café located? 2) What types of food do you serve? 3) Do you have any vegetarian dishes? 4) Is there a dancing floor? 5) Do you offer any discounts for groups? Analyse the inversion in each; let them ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question.

Task 3 · Interview (5 questions)

15 min

Answer 5 interview questions on the "birthday & celebrations" topic. Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer. (In the real exam you hear the questions — the audio is below.)

Questions (real FIPI topic):
  1. How do you usually celebrate your birthday?
  2. What birthday traditions do you have in your family?
  3. What's the best birthday present you've received?
  4. Do you prefer your birthday presents to be a surprise, or do you tell people what you want? Why?
  5. What is your best birthday memory? Why?
Technique. No "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, usually, because, for example and the topical phrases (exchange gifts, a festive atmosphere, gather around the table). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences.
0:40
Common mark losses (FIPI-2024): a ONE-phrase answer instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer doesn't match the question (what do you usually do → answer I'll…); a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of a precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer (Cake.).
Model answer (question 2): «In my family we have a deep-rooted tradition of starting the day with a special breakfast that my mum prepares. Then everyone gives me their presents one by one, and in the evening we all gather around the table for a big dinner together.» Note the "answer → detail → example" structure and the topical phrases. Let them answer all 5 in 40 sec, for the recording. (Real FIPI Task 3 audio & questions — birthday traditions.)

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The most "valuable" task (max marks). The project "The best New Year holiday" — leave a voice message to a friend about two photos, speak continuously for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — do ALL in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences;
  • ② 1–2 advantages of the two ways of spending the New Year holiday;
  • ③ 1–2 disadvantages of the two ways;
  • ④ your opinion: which way of spending the New Year holiday you'd prefer and why.
Supports (monologue frame):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our project "The best New Year holiday"…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that…
• One advantage of … is that…; another benefit is…
• However, a drawback of … is that…
• As for me, I'd rather spend New Year… because…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): task completion — all 4 points covered; organisation — introduction, logic, ending, linkers; language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Main mistakes in task 4 (FIPI-2024): a superficial difference (in photo 1 it's snowy, in photo 2 it's indoors) instead of a generalisation about the essence (a quiet family New Year vs a lively party); photos not linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a coherent monologue; a shift of genre into "storytelling"; skipping a plan point; long pauses.
Teacher’s note: first build the frame together (using the supports), then record the whole thing for 3 min without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak continuously. Link it to the topic: two contrasting ways of celebrating New Year → the role of family vs. festive crowds. (Real FIPI Task 4 — "The best New Year holiday" project, two photos.)
