Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently completes all 4 speaking tasks of the EGE oral exam (tasks 1–4) on the topic of nature, weather and ecology, applying the vocabulary from Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: a warm-up recycling the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled speech (asking questions, an interview about ecology) → to free monologue (comparing two photos) — with growing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we drill "model → support → own performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI marking and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write out 5 phrases on the topic of nature and ecology from Lessons 1–2. For each one add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning.

Step 2 · Share in pairs (Pair Share, 5 min).

Partner A tells their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 clarifying question and adds a phrase the partner did not have. Then you swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • What do you personally do to go green and cut down on waste?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (each student writes 5 phrases ON THEIR OWN — this consolidates far better than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to bring the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (carbon footprint, go green, renewable energy sources, pose a serious threat to, make a difference…) into active speech before the speaking tasks. Put the best phrases on the board — they will feed into tasks 3–4.

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it silently to yourself), then read it aloud. The marks are for phonetics: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make meaningful pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and when listing. ③ Link words (linking): an_apple, there‿is. ④ Watch out for the "tricky" words of the topic: nature, climate, environment, mountain, island (silent s), comfortable, area, desert.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common lost marks (FIPI-2024 data): a pause INSIDE a phrasal verb (looking · for) — breaks the meaning; dropping the linking r (there‿is); stressing the unstressed prefix a- (ˈalike instead of aˈlike, aˈpart, aˈbove); flat intonation in a list (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); noun↔verb stress (ˈrecord vs reˈcord); inserting extra articles/prepositions that aren't in the text.
Teacher’s note: the task 1 text is general-knowledge (phonetics is tested on any text, the topic is not tied to it). Have them read it twice, the second time on a recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload — phonetics is marked "spot by spot".
3

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

You are planning a hike in the mountains and want to find out the details. Study the travel agency advert and ask 5 direct questions about the points. The marks are for the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and how well it fits the point.

Ask about: 1) duration of the tour; 2) dates of the tour; 3) minimum age; 4) special equipment; 5) price for one.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb? — How long DOES the tour last? What ARE the dates? IS any special equipment needed? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Main mistakes in task 2 (FIPI-2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a one-size-fits-all template (Do you have information about…?) — are NOT counted; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixed structures (Where is the duration of…?); a question not asked for all 5 points. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EVERY point.
Model questions (FIPI): 1) How long does the tour last? 2) What are the dates of the tour? 3) What is the minimum age for the tour? 4) Is any special equipment needed? 5) How much does the tour cost for one person? Go through the inversion in each one; have them ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question.

Task 3 · Interview (5 questions)

15 min

Answer 5 interview questions on the topic of "environmental problems". Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer.

Questions (real FIPI topic — environmental problems):
  1. What do you think of environmental problems? Are these important personally for you?
  2. What do you think is the most dangerous environmental problem of our time? Why?
  3. Are there any ecological problems in the place where you live? What can you tell us about them?
  4. Are there any ecological activist groups in your region? What do they do?
  5. Do you agree that all of us should contribute to solving environmental problems?
Technique. Not "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, mostly, because, for example and the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (carbon footprint, air pollution, go green). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences. The verb tense in the answer must match the question.
0:40
Common lost marks (FIPI-2024): an answer of ONE phrase instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer doesn't match the question; a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer (Pollution.).
Model answers (FIPI):
1) «I think environmental problems are some of the most serious issues we face today, and they definitely matter to me personally. I try to do small things like recycling and saving water, because I believe our generation will have to deal with the consequences.»
2) «In my opinion, climate change is the most dangerous environmental problem of our time. It causes extreme weather, melting ice and rising sea levels, and unlike many other issues it affects the entire planet at the same time.»
3) «Yes, there are some ecological problems where I live, mainly air pollution from cars and rubbish that people leave in parks. The city is trying to add more green spaces, but I think much more still needs to be done.»
4–5) «Yes, there are a few volunteer groups in my region that organise clean-up days and plant trees. They also run campaigns in schools, and I completely agree that all of us should contribute, because no single person can solve these problems alone.»
Draw attention to the "answer → reason → example" structure and the topic vocabulary. Have them answer all 5 in 40 sec each, on a recording.
5

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The most "valuable" task (max marks). The "Weather" project — leave a voice message for a friend about two photos with different weather, speak continuously for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — do them ALL in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences (in terms of the "weather" topic, not "in photo 1 there's a girl, in photo 2 a boy");
  • ② 1–2 advantages of each kind of weather;
  • ③ 1–2 drawbacks of each kind of weather;
  • ④ your opinion: which weather you prefer and why.
Support (monologue frame):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our project on weather…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that…
• One advantage of … weather is that…; another benefit is…
• However, a drawback of … is that…
• As for me, I prefer… because…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): K1 content — all 4 points covered; K2 organisation — introduction, logic, conclusion, linkers; K3 language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Main mistakes in task 4 (FIPI-2024): a superficial difference (in photo 1 it's sunny, in photo 2 it's raining) instead of a generalisation in terms of the topic (warm vs cold / clear vs stormy weather and its effect on life); photos not linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a coherent monologue; switching genre to a "story"; skipping a plan point; long pauses.
Teacher’s note: first build the frame together (using the support), then a full recording for 3 min without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak continuously. Link it to the course topic: weather → climate → extreme weather conditions (Lesson 1), so the student weaves the topic vocabulary into the monologue.
