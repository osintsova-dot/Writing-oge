3

Grammar · "Use of English" (tasks 19–31)

33 min
Don't confuse the two tasks (although both say "transform the word in CAPITALS"): 3A tasks 19–24 — you change only the grammatical form, the part of speech stays the same (work → worked, good → better, child → children). 3B tasks 25–31 — you form a NEW word of a different part of speech (fame → famous, decide → decision). Tip: 3A — "which form?", 3B — "which part of speech?".
3A · Grammatical forms (tasks 19–24).

Put the word in CAPITALS into the correct grammatical form — the part of speech does NOT change. What is tested:

What is testedExample
Verb tense / form (incl. passive)get → got (Past Simple), concentrate → is concentrated (Passive)
Degrees of comparison (adj./adv.)good → best, easy → easier, far → further
Pronouns (subject/possessive/object)they → them, I → my
Plural, incl. irregularschild → children, life → lives
Numerals (ordinal)one → first, two → second
Practice · real FIPI tasks. Put the word into the correct form:
  • 1. You see, setting an example is the way of teaching. (good)
  • 2. About 90% of the world's fresh water in Antarctica. (concentrate)
  • 3. Any planet with an atmosphere can have auroras around . (they)
  • 4. Few people know that Sofia interested in mathematics at a very early age. (get)
  • 5. The speech was so captivating that for the time in their lives the reporters forgot to take notes. (one)
  • 6. love making New Year cards and giving them to their parents. (child)
Answers 3A: 1 best · 2 is concentrated · 3 them · 4 got · 5 first · 6 Children. Note: 2 — passive (the water "is concentrated"), 4 — Past Simple of an irregular verb, 6 — plural + capital letter (start of the sentence). The part of speech never changed — that is exactly what distinguishes 3A from 3B.
3B · Word formation (tasks 25–31).

You are given a word from the same root — form the required part of speech: ① work out the part of speech from its position in the sentence; ② choose the suffix/prefix; ③ check the meaning (often a negative prefix is needed) and the number.

Part of speechCommon suffixes / prefixesExamples
Noun-tion/-sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ence, -ist, -eraccommodate → accommodation, happy → happiness
Adjective-ful, -ous, -ive, -al, -able, -ic, -lessfame → famous, colour → colourful
Adverb-ly (from an adjective)real → really
Negation (prefix)un-, in-, im-, ir-, il-, dis-, non-, inter-inhabited → uninhabited, national → international
Practice · real FIPI tasks. Form the word:
  • 1. The University of Auckland has a strong focus on legal and programmes for students from all over the world. (busy)
  • 2. at the resort ranges from budget to luxury. (accommodate)
  • 3. Whistler is a two-hour drive from Vancouver and is a ski resort. (fame)
  • 4. The streets look magical because they are illuminated with numerous lights. (colour)
  • 5. , free guided tours depart from the entrance hall. (alternative)
  • 6. If you are lucky to see the Northern Lights, you will be impressed. (real)
  • 7. By the age of 19, Tretyak was famous not only in Russia, but also on the level. (nation)
  • 8. You can go on day trips to the remote, islands where no one lives. (inhabited)
Answers 3B: 1 business · 2 Accommodation · 3 famous · 4 colourful · 5 Alternatively · 6 really · 7 international · 8 uninhabited. Traps: 7 — you need the PREFIX inter-; 8 — the negative prefix un- because of "where no one lives"; busi-ness and fam-ous drop the "e"/"y". 4

Writing · Email to a friend (task 37)

40 min

🔗 Bridge. The forms and word formation we have just practised are exactly what "language accuracy" (K3) means. Now we apply it where it earns marks — in a real email about Russian holidays.

✉️ Real EGE task. You have received an email from your pen-friend. Write a reply.

From: Kelly@mail.uk  ·  To: Russian_friend@ege.ru  ·  Subject: Russian traditions

…They say that Russian New Year traditions are unique. How do Russians celebrate the New Year? What are some special New Year dishes? How does your family celebrate the New Year? I've decided to start learning Russian…

Write an email to Kelly. In your message:
– answer her 3 questions; – ask 3 questions about her decision to start learning Russian. Write 100–140 words. Remember the rules of email writing.

4.1 · Structure (5 parts).
1Greeting + opening. "Dear Kelly," + a reaction to her news / thanks for the email (1–2 sentences). 2Answer her 3 questions. A separate paragraph — answer ALL three questions, 1–2 sentences each, with details and B2 vocabulary. 3Ask 3 questions. A separate paragraph — ask 3 questions specifically about her decision to start learning Russian (not general ones!). 4Closing. A closing phrase + "Best wishes," / "Write back soon," + your name on a new line.
4.2 · Useful phrases.
• Thanks for your email!
• It was great to hear from you.
• What an exciting decision!
• In Russia we usually celebrate the New Year by…
• A traditional New Year dish is…
• In my family we always… / it's a deep-rooted tradition to…
• Speaking of Russian, I'd love to know…
• Why…? How long…? Do you find…?
• That's all for now.
• Write back soon and tell me…
Criteria (max 6 marks): K1 content — all 3 answers + 3 on-topic questions, correct format; K2 organisation — salutation, logical paragraphs, linkers, closing; K3 language — accuracy and range of vocabulary/grammar (this is where the Lesson 1 holiday phrases go!).
4.3 · Editing (K3): find and correct the mistake. Typical B2 mistakes in a holidays email — write the correct version:
  • 1. No one knows exactly when this winter tradition founded. →
  • 2. Every winter thousands of people take part on the street celebrations. → take part
  • 3. The midnight fireworks show was so excited that nobody wanted to leave. →
  • 4. For me, New Year is the most beautifulest holiday of all. → the
  • 5. Every region in Russia keeps many old tradition alive. → many old
  • 6. In the past, we use to gather at my grandparents' house. → we
Answers 4.3: 1 was founded · 2 in · 3 exciting · 4 most beautiful · 5 traditions · 6 used to. Error type (typical B2 mistakes that cost strong students the K3 mark): 1 passive voice (the tradition is acted upon → was founded, NOT "found/founded"); 2 verb + preposition rection (take part IN); 3 -ed / -ing adjective (the show is "exciting", people feel "excited"); 4 superlative form (no double "most + -est"); 5 countable plural after "many" (traditions); 6 used to + infinitive for a past habit (not "use to"). Have students hunt for the SAME six types in their own email before handing it in.
4.4 · Write your email.
0 words
Model answer (≈125 words):
Dear Kelly,
Thanks for your email! It's wonderful that you find our traditions so interesting.
In Russia we usually celebrate the New Year with the whole family: we decorate a tree, exchange gifts and gather around the table just before midnight. A traditional New Year dish is "Olivier" salad, and we always have tangerines and a lavish feast. In my family it's a deep-rooted tradition to make a wish as the clock strikes twelve and then watch the fireworks.
By the way, your decision to learn Russian sounds exciting! Why did you choose Russian? How long have you been studying it? Do you find the alphabet difficult?
That's all for now. Write back soon and tell me about your progress!
Best wishes,
Alex
Walk-through with the student: point out the 3 answers + 3 questions (K1), the paragraphs and linkers (K2), the phrases exchange gifts, gather around the table, a lavish feast, a deep-rooted tradition (K3). Then let them write their own email and submit it for AI marking in the trainer.
