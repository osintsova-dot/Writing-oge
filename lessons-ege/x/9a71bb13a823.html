Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently performs all 4 tasks of the EGE speaking part (tasks 1–4) on the topic "Media, gadgets and communication", applying the vocabulary from Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: a warm-up recycling the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled (questions, interview) → to a free monologue (comparing photos) — with growing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we drill "model → support → own performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI marking and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write down 5 phrases/idioms on the topic "Media" from Lessons 1–2. For each, add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning.

Step 2 · Share in pairs (Pair Share, 5 min).

Partner A presents their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 clarifying question and adds a phrase the partner did not have. Then you swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • Do you trust the news you see on social media? How do you fact-check a story?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (each student writes 5 phrases ON THEIR OWN — this consolidates more strongly than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to bring the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 into active speech before the oral tasks. Put strong phrases (a reliable source, to spread misinformation, to go viral) on the board — they will feed into tasks 3–4.

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it silently), then you read it aloud. Phonetics is assessed: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make meaningful pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and in lists. ③ Link words: an_article, read_it. ④ Watch out for "tricky" words: comfortable, vegetable, recipe, mountain, island (silent s), determine, area, audience.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common loss of marks (FIPI-2024 data): a pause INSIDE a phrasal verb (looking · for) — it breaks the meaning; dropping the linking r (there‿is); stress on the prefix a- (ˈapart instead of aˈpart, aˈlike); flat intonation in a list (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); adding/dropping extra articles and endings that aren't in the text.
Teacher’s note: have them read it twice, the second time for a recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload — phonetics is marked "spot by spot". (Real FIPI text for reading aloud, task 1.)
3

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

Study the advert and ask 4 direct questions on the prompts. Assessed: the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and how well it matches the prompt.

You are considering buying a new tablet. Ask about: 1) available brands; 2) the largest screen size; 3) price range; 4) discounts.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb? — What brands ARE available? What size DO you offer? DO you have any discounts? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Main mistakes in task 2 (FIPI-2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a generic template (Do you have information about…?) — NOT credited; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixing constructions (Where is the price of…?); which instead of what where there is no set list; a question not asked on all 4 prompts. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EACH prompt.
Model questions (FIPI): 1) What brands are available? 2) What is the largest screen size you offer? 3) What is the price range? 4) Do you offer any discounts? Analyse the inversion in each; have them ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question. (Real FIPI advert, task 2 — "Gadgets for all ages", tablets.)

Task 3 · Interview (5 questions)

15 min

Answer 5 interview questions on the topic "phones and media". Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer.

Questions (real FIPI topic):
  1. How long have you had your mobile phone?
  2. What do you usually use your mobile phone for?
  3. How do you feel if you forget your mobile phone at home?
  4. Where should you switch off your mobile phone?
  5. Why is it difficult to live without a mobile phone now?
Technique. Not "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, mostly, because, for example and topic vocabulary (to scroll endlessly, screen time, to keep up with the news). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences. Watch out: the verb tense in your answer must match the question.
0:40
Common loss of marks (FIPI-2024): an answer in ONE phrase instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer doesn't match the question (what do you usually use… → answer I'll use…); a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of a precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer (For chatting.).
Model answer (question 5): "It's difficult to live without a mobile phone now because our whole lives are connected to it. We use it for communication, navigation, payments and even studying, so without it we would feel completely lost. Society has become so dependent on these devices that simple daily tasks would suddenly become much harder." Note the "answer → reason → example" structure and the linkers. Have them answer all 5 at 40 sec each, for a recording. (Real FIPI task, task 3 — mobile phones.)
5

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The most "valuable" task (max marks). Project "Communication" — leave a voice message to a friend about two photos, speak continuously for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — cover ALL in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences (in essence — two different ways of communicating);
  • ② 1–2 advantages of the two ways of communicating;
  • ③ 1–2 disadvantages of the two ways of communicating;
  • ④ your opinion: which way of communicating you prefer and why.
Support (monologue framework):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our project on communication…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that one is face-to-face and the other is online…
• One advantage of … is that…; another benefit is…
• However, a drawback of … is that… (you can fall for misinformation / be glued to the screen)
• As for me, I prefer… because…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): task achievement — all 4 points covered; organisation — opening, logic, ending, linkers; language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Main mistakes in task 4 (FIPI-2024): a superficial difference (photo 1 shows a girl with a phone, photo 2 shows two people talking) instead of a generalisation about the essence of the topic (online vs face-to-face communication); photos not linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a coherent monologue; switching genre to a "story"; skipping a plan point; long pauses.
Teacher’s note: first build the framework together (using the support phrases), then record the whole thing for 3 min without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak continuously. Link it to the topic: online communication and media → reliability, echo chamber, screen time. (Real FIPI task, task 4 — project "Communication".)
