Grammar · "Use of English" (tasks 19–31)

33 min
Don't confuse the two tasks (even though both say "transform the word in CAPITALS"): 3A tasks 19–24 — change only the grammatical form, the part of speech stays the same (work → worked, good → better, child → children). 3B tasks 25–31 — form a NEW word of a different part of speech (fame → famous, decide → decision). Tip: 3A — "which form?", 3B — "which part of speech?".
3A · Grammatical forms (tasks 19–24).

The word in CAPITALS must be put into the correct grammatical form — the part of speech does NOT change. What is tested:

What is testedExample
Verb tense / form (incl. passive)get → got (Past Simple), concentrate → is concentrated (Passive)
Degrees of comparison (adj./adv.)good → best, easy → easier, far → further
Pronouns (subject/possessive/object)they → them, I → my
Plural, incl. irregularschild → children, life → lives
Numerals (ordinal)one → first, two → second
Practice · real FIPI tasks. Put the word into the correct form:
  • 1. You see, setting an example is the way of teaching. (good)
  • 2. About 90% of the world's fresh water in Antarctica. (concentrate)
  • 3. Any planet with an atmosphere can have auroras around . (they)
  • 4. Few people know that Sofia interested in mathematics at a very early age. (get)
  • 5. The speech was so captivating that for the time in their lives the reporters forgot to take notes. (one)
  • 6. love making New Year cards and giving them to their parents. (child)
Answers 3A: 1 best · 2 is concentrated · 3 them · 4 got · 5 first · 6 Children. Note: 2 — passive, 4 — Past Simple of an irregular verb, 6 — plural + capital letter (start of sentence). The part of speech never changed — that is what sets 3A apart from 3B. Real FIPI tasks (ege_grammar.json), not tied to the topic.
3B · Word formation (tasks 25–31).

You are given a related word — form the required part of speech: ① identify the part of speech from its place in the sentence; ② choose the suffix/prefix; ③ check the meaning (often a negative prefix is needed) and number.

Part of speechCommon suffixes / prefixesExamples
Noun-tion/-sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ence, -ist, -eraccommodate → accommodation, happy → happiness
Adjective-ful, -ous, -ive, -al, -able, -ic, -lessfame → famous, colour → colourful
Adverb-ly (from an adjective)real → really
Negation (prefix)un-, in-, im-, ir-, il-, dis-, non-, inter-inhabited → uninhabited, national → international

  • 2. at the resort ranges from budget to luxury. (accommodate)
  • 3. Whistler is a two-hour drive from Vancouver and is a ski resort. (fame)
  • 4. The streets look magical because they are illuminated with numerous lights. (colour)
  • 5. , free guided tours depart from the entrance hall. (alternative)
  • 6. If you are lucky to see the Northern Lights, you will be impressed. (real)
  • 7. By the age of 19, Tretyak was famous not only in Russia, but also on the level. (nation)

Traps: 7 — the PREFIX inter- is needed; 8 — the negative prefix un- because of the meaning "where no one lives"; busi-ness and fam-ous drop the "e"/"y". Many of the items here are about travelling (resort, accommodation, guided tours, uninhabited islands). Real FIPI tasks (ege_lexis.json). Full practice — in the trainer (homework).
4

Writing · Email to a friend (task 37)

40 min

🔗 Bridge. The forms and word formation we have just practised are exactly the "language accuracy" of K3. Now we apply it where it earns marks — in a real email to a friend about travelling.

✉️ Real EGE task. You have received an email from a pen-friend. Write back to them.

From: Olive@mail.uk  ·  To: Russian_friend@ege.ru  ·  Subject: St. Petersburg

…At college we are doing projects on the historic cities of the world. If I choose St. Petersburg in Russia, what places of interest should I write about? Is St. Petersburg popular among foreign and local tourists, and why? What season is the best to visit St. Petersburg? We've just returned from the trip to the seaside…

Write an email to Olive. In your message:
– answer her 3 questions; – ask 3 questions about her trip to the seaside. Write 100–140 words. Remember the rules of email writing.

4.1 · Structure (5 parts).
1Greeting + opening. "Dear Olive," + a reaction to the news / thanks for the email (1–2 sentences). 2Answer her 3 questions. A separate paragraph — answer ALL three questions (places, popularity with tourists + why, best season), 1–2 sentences each, with details and B2 vocabulary. 3Ask 3 questions. A separate paragraph — ask 3 questions specifically about her trip to the seaside (not general ones!). 4Closing. A closing line + "Best wishes," / "Write back soon," + your name on a new line.
4.2 · Useful phrases.
• Thanks for your email!
• It was great to hear from you.
• Your project sounds really interesting!
• You should definitely write about… (the Hermitage, Nevsky Prospect).
• It's a must-see attraction for…
• It's hugely popular with tourists because…
• The best season to visit is… because…
• By the way, I've got a few questions about your trip.
• Where exactly…? How long…? Did you…?
• Write back soon and tell me…
Criteria (max 6 marks): K1 content — all 3 answers + 3 questions on topic, correct format; K2 organisation — salutation, logical paragraphs, linkers, closing; K3 language — accuracy and range of vocabulary/grammar (this is where the Lesson 1 idioms go!).
4.3 · Editing (K3): find and correct the error. Typical B2 errors in a travel email — write the correct version:
  • 1. Could you tell me where is the station? →
  • 2. I'm looking forward to visit Italy this summer. →
  • 3. We arrived to London in the morning. →
  • 4. Last year we have travelled to Spain. →
  • 5. We didn't take much suitcases. →
  • 6. The hotel was enough comfortable for us. →
Answers (K3, B2): 1 where the station is; 2 visiting; 3 in; 4 travelled; 5 many; 6 comfortable enough. Error type: 1 indirect question; 2 gerund after to; 3 arrive IN a city; 4 Past Simple with last year; 5 many with countable nouns; 6 adjective + enough.
4.4 · Write your email.
0 words
Model answer (≈125 words):
Dear Olive,
Thanks for your email! Your project on historic cities sounds really exciting.
If you choose St. Petersburg, you should definitely write about the Hermitage, Nevsky Prospect and the breathtaking view from St. Isaac's colonnade — they are real must-see attractions. The city is hugely popular with both foreign and local tourists, mainly because of its imperial palaces and romantic canals. In my opinion, the best season to visit is summer, during the famous «White Nights», when it never really gets dark.
By the way, I'd love to hear about your seaside trip. Where exactly did you go? How long did you stay there? Did you try any water sports?
That's all for now. Write back soon and tell me everything!
Best wishes,
Alex
Walk-through with the student: point out the 3 answers + 3 questions (K1), the paragraphs and linkers (K2), the idioms breathtaking view, must-see attraction (K3). Have them write their own email and submit it for AI marking in the trainer.
