Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently completes all 4 tasks of the EGE oral part (tasks 1–4) using the topic "hobbies & free time", applying the vocabulary from Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: vocabulary-recycling warm-up (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled (questions, interview) → to free monologue (comparing photos) — with growing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we practise "model → support → own performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI assessment and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write out 5 phrases on the topic "hobbies & free time" from Lessons 1–2. For each one add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning.

Step 2 · Pair Share (5 min).

Partner A says their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 follow-up question and adds a phrase the partner didn't have. Then swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • How do you usually spend your free time — alone or with friends?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (each student writes 5 phrases ON THEIR OWN — this consolidates more strongly than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to lift the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (take up a hobby, unwind, a creative outlet, strike a healthy balance) into active speech before the oral tasks. Note good phrases on the board — they'll feed into tasks 3–4.

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it silently), then read it aloud. The mark is for phonetics: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make sense-group pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and when listing. ③ Link words (linking): an_apple, read_it. ④ Watch out for "tricky" words: comfortable, vegetable, recipe, mountain, island (silent s), determine, area, leisure, hobbies.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common mark losses (FIPI 2024 data): a pause INSIDE a sense group or a phrasal verb — it breaks the meaning; missing linking (there‿is); wrong word stress in long words; flat intonation in a list (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); adding/dropping articles and prepositions that aren't in the text; "Russian" pronunciation of the -ed/-tion endings.
Teacher’s note: let them read it twice, the second time for the recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload — phonetics is marked "selectively". The image-text is real FIPI (Open Bank, oral task 1); the phonetics task is thematically neutral.

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

Study the advert for painting classes and ask 5 direct questions on the given points. The mark is for the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and how well it matches the point.

«The best painting classes now in your area!» Ask about: 1) location; 2) number of classes a week; 3) styles of painting taught; 4) timetable; 5) tuition fee.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb? — Where ARE the classes held? How many classes ARE there a week? What styles DO you teach? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Main errors in task 2 (FIPI 2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a one-size-fits-all template (Do you have information about…?) — do NOT count; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixed structures (Where is the location of…?); the question isn't asked for all 5 points. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EACH point.
Model questions (FIPI, zid 5F3D71): 1) Where are the painting classes held? 2) How many classes are there a week? 3) What styles of painting do you teach? 4) What is the timetable? 5) How much is the tuition fee? Go through the inversion in each; have them ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question.

Task 3 · Interview

15 min

Answer the interview questions on the topic "free time and weekends". Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer.

🎧 The examiner's voice (real FIPI recording) — you can listen and answer in the pauses:

Questions (real FIPI task, zid 1F217B):
  1. How do you usually spend your weekend?
  2. What are some popular weekend activities among your friends?
  3. What are your plans for the coming weekend?
  4. How do you feel when the weekend is over? What would you like to change about your typical weekend?
Technique. Not "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, mostly, because, for example and topic phrases (Lesson 1: unwind, recharge my batteries, a couch potato). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences.
0:40
Common mark losses (FIPI 2024): a ONE-phrase answer instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer doesn't match the question (How do you usually spend… → answer I'll…); a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of a precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer (Gaming.).
Model answer (question 1, FIPI): «I usually spend my weekend catching up on sleep and doing things I don't have time for during the week. I like to meet my friends, go for a walk, and in the evening I unwind by watching series or playing video games.» Note the "answer → details → example" structure and the topic phrase. Have them answer all the questions in 40 sec, for the recording. (The real FIPI set has 4 points; the last is a double one, effectively 5 prompts.)
5

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The most "valuable" task (max marks). The "Hobbies" project — leave a voice message to a friend about two photos, speaking without stopping for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — do them ALL in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences (justify your choice of photos for the project);
  • ② 1–2 advantages of the two hobbies;
  • ③ 1–2 disadvantages of the two hobbies;
  • ④ your opinion: which of these hobbies you would prefer and why.
Support (monologue framework):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our "Hobbies" project…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that…
• One advantage of … is that it helps you unwind / broaden your horizons…
• However, a drawback of … is that…
• As for me, I'd prefer… because it's a real creative outlet for me…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): task completion — all 4 points covered; organisation — introduction, logic, ending, linkers; language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Main errors in task 4 (FIPI 2024): a surface difference (in photo 1 there's one person, in photo 2 a group) instead of a generalisation on the essence of the "hobbies" topic; the photos aren't linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a connected monologue; switching the genre to "a story"; missing a plan point; long pauses.
Teacher’s note (zid 175951, "Hobbies" project): first build the framework together (from the support phrases), then a full recording for 3 min without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak without breaks. Link it to Lesson 1 vocabulary: a creative outlet, broaden your horizons, strike a healthy balance, glued to a screen — this raises the "language" mark.
