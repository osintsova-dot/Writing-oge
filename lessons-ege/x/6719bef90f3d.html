Lesson 3 — Speaking · B2The oral exam: from reading aloud to a full monologue

Lesson outcome: the student confidently performs all 4 tasks of the EGE speaking paper (tasks 1–4) on the topic of "school / studying / subjects", applying the vocabulary from Lessons 1–2. Lesson flow: warm-up recycling the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 (Write-Pair-Share) → from controlled speech (reading aloud) → to semi-controlled (questions, interview) → to free monologue (comparing the "School subjects" photos) — with increasing independence. Through-line: each task has its own technique and its own criteria; we practise "model → support → independent performance with a recording". Homework: "Speaking" in the EGE trainer (recording + AI marking and model answers).
1

Warm-up · Recall & share (Write-Pair-Share)

10 min
Step 1 · Write (3 min).

Recall and write down 5 phrases/idioms on the topic of "school and studying" from Lessons 1–2. For each, add an example sentence and a short explanation of the meaning.

Step 2 · Share in pairs (Pair Share, 5 min).

Partner A says their 5 phrases with examples — Partner B listens, asks 1 clarifying question and adds a phrase the partner did not have. Then swap roles.

Step 3 · Quick discussion (2 min).
  • Which school subject do you find the most useful for your future — and why?
  • What is the hardest part of the oral exam for you — and why?
Teacher’s note — Kagan Write-Pair-Share: first individual retrieval practice (each student writes 5 phrases ON THEIR OWN — this fixes them more strongly than "let's recall together"), then Pair Share (partners take turns sharing, the listener adds/asks). The aim is to bring the vocabulary of Lessons 1–2 into active speech before the speaking tasks. Note the strong phrases (compulsory/elective subject, broaden one's horizons, packed timetable) on the board — they will feed into tasks 3–4.
2

Task 1 · Reading aloud

15 min

1.5 min to prepare (read it silently), then read aloud. The assessment focuses on phonology: pronunciation, stress, pauses and intonation.

Technique. ① Make meaningful pauses at commas/full stops, don't "stumble". ② Intonation: ↘ in statements and wh-questions, ↗ in yes/no questions and when listing. ③ Link the words (linking): an_apple, read_it. ④ Watch out for "tricky" words: comfortable, vegetable, knowledge, recipe, determine, area, subtle.

🔎 Real FIPI text. Read it silently, then aloud within 1.5 minutes:

1:30
Common loss of marks (FIPI-2024 data): a pause INSIDE a phrasal verb (looking · for) — breaks the meaning; dropping the linking r (there‿is); stressing the prefix a- (ˈalike instead of aˈlike, aˈpart); shifting noun↔verb stress (ˈrecord noun / re­ˈcord verb); flat intonation when listing (you need ↗ on the first items, ↘ on the last); inserting/dropping extra articles and endings not in the text.
Teacher’s note: let them read it twice, the second time for the recording. Mark 2–3 words for pronunciation and 1 spot for intonation. Don't overload them — phonology is marked "spot by spot". Task 1 assesses phonology, not topic, so the text is general informative content (a real FIPI text for reading aloud).
3

Task 2 · Asking questions about an advert

15 min

Study the advert "Individual language classes with native speakers!" and ask 5 direct questions on the prompts. The assessment focuses on the grammatical accuracy of the direct question and its relevance to the prompt.

Ask about: 1) location; 2) price for one lesson; 3) duration of the lesson; 4) languages available; 5) evening classes.

Direct question technique. Pattern: (Wh) + auxiliary verb + subject + main verb? — Where ARE the classes held? How much DOES one lesson cost? ARE there any evening classes? IMPORTANT: this is a direct question (with inversion), NOT an indirect one.
0:20
Top mistakes in task 2 (FIPI-2024): a polite/indirect question (Could you tell me…?) and a one-size-fits-all template (Do you have information about…?) — do NOT count; ellipsis (What about the price?); mixing structures (Where is the location of…?); a question not asked for all 5 prompts. You need a DIRECT question with inversion for EACH prompt.
Model questions (FIPI): 1) Where are the classes held? 2) How much does one lesson cost? 3) How long does a lesson last? 4) What languages are available? 5) Are there any evening classes? Analyse the inversion in each; let them ask their own aloud at 20 sec/question.
4

Task 3 · Interview

15 min

Answer the interview questions on the topic of "reading and studying". Give full answers (2–3 sentences), 40 seconds per answer.

Questions (real FIPI topic — Reading & learning):
  1. Do you agree that nowadays people read less than they used to in the past?
  2. What is better, to read a book or to watch its screened version? Why?
  3. How can good reading skills help teenagers in their future career? Who or what can help children shape their reading habits?
  4. What are your current reading preferences? Have they changed since you were a child?
Technique. No "yes/no" — give 2–3 sentences: answer + reason/example. Use I'd say…, It depends, mostly, because, for example and the study vocabulary (Lesson 1). 40 seconds = ~3 sentences. Make sure the verb tense in your answer matches the question.
0:40
Common loss of marks (FIPI-2024): a ONE-phrase answer instead of two; no reason (Why?); the verb tense in the answer not matching the question; a memorised "topic" of 7–8 sentences instead of a precise 2–3 on the question; a one-word answer.
Model answer (question 3, from the FIPI bank): "Good reading skills help teenagers process information quickly and think critically, which is essential in almost any profession. Whether you become a lawyer, an engineer or a doctor, you constantly have to read complex documents and reports. As for habits, I believe parents play the biggest role, but inspiring teachers and even friends who recommend books can make a huge difference too." Note the "answer → reason → example" structure and the collocation a supportive teacher (Lesson 1). Let them answer all the questions within 40 sec, for the recording.
5

Task 4 · Voice message comparing two photos

30 min

The highest-value task (max marks). The "School subjects" project — leave a voice message for a friend about two photos, speaking non-stop for ~3 min (12–15 sentences).

Plan (4 points — cover ALL in order):
  • ① briefly describe both photos and name the differences between the two school subjects;
  • ② 1–2 advantages of the two subjects;
  • ③ 1–2 disadvantages of the two subjects;
  • ④ your opinion: which of these subjects you preferred at school and why.
Support (monologue framework):
• Hi! I've found two great photos for our project on school subjects…
• The first photo shows… while the second one shows…
• The main difference is that one subject is more practical/creative, whereas the other is more theoretical…
• One advantage of … is that it helps you broaden your horizons; another benefit is…
• However, a drawback of … is that…
• As for me, at school I preferred… because…
• That's all for now. Talk to you soon!
3:00
Criteria (max 7 marks): task achievement — all 4 points covered; organisation — introduction, logic, ending, linkers; language — range and accuracy. Silence/unfinished points = lost marks.
Top mistakes in task 4 (FIPI-2024): a superficial difference (in photo 1 there's a girl, in photo 2 a boy) instead of a generalisation about the essence (a practical vs a theoretical subject); photos not linked to the project topic; point-by-point answers instead of a coherent monologue; switching genre to a "story"; skipping a plan point; long pauses.
Teacher’s note: first build the framework together (using the supports), then a full 3-minute recording without stopping. The key is to cover ALL 4 points and speak non-stop. Link it to the lesson topic: compulsory/elective subjects, how they broaden one's horizons.
